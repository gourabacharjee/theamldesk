Careers in AML

How to break into anti-money laundering compliance — certifications, job roles, interview prep, and resume tips.

Opportunity

Why consider an AML career?

AML/compliance is one of the fastest-growing fields in financial services. As regulations tighten globally and fines reach billions of dollars, banks, fintechs, and consulting firms are hiring compliance professionals at scale.

📈

Growing Demand

Every regulated financial institution must have an AML programme. Compliance headcount has grown 10–15% annually in major banks.

🌐

Global Mobility

AML skills are transferable across jurisdictions. FATF standards are global, so your knowledge applies in London, New York, Singapore, or Mumbai.

💰

Competitive Pay

Compliance professionals command competitive salaries, especially with certifications like CAMS and experience in EDD or transaction monitoring.

🎯

Purpose-Driven Work

AML work directly combats financial crime — drug trafficking, terrorism, corruption, and fraud. It's meaningful work with real impact.

Job Roles

Common entry-level AML roles

🔍 KYC/CDD Analyst

Performs customer due diligence — verifying identity, assessing risk, collecting documentation. Often the first point of entry into AML.

Skills needed: Attention to detail, understanding of KYC/CDD requirements, research skills, data entry accuracy.

📊 Transaction Monitoring Analyst

Reviews automated alerts from transaction monitoring systems, investigates potential suspicious activity, and writes SAR narratives.

Skills needed: Analytical thinking, understanding of ML typologies, clear writing (SAR narratives), ability to work with large datasets.

🎯 Sanctions Screening Analyst

Reviews sanctions alerts (name matches, country matches), performs research to confirm or dismiss matches, and escalates confirmed hits.

Skills needed: Knowledge of OFAC/UN/EU sanctions regimes, research skills, ability to work under time pressure.

📝 Compliance Associate / Officer

Broader compliance role covering policy development, regulatory change management, training, and risk assessment. Often requires 2–3 years of experience.

Skills needed: Regulatory knowledge, communication, policy writing, stakeholder management.

Credentials

Key certifications

Gold Standard

CAMS — Certified Anti-Money Laundering Specialist

Provider: ACAMS (Association of Certified Anti-Money Laundering Specialists)

The most widely recognized AML certification globally. Covers AML/CFT principles, compliance standards, and best practices. Highly valued by employers worldwide.

Eligibility: 40 qualification credits (combination of education and experience). Freshers with a master's degree qualify.

ICA Certifications

Provider: International Compliance Association (in partnership with University of Manchester)

Offers the ICA Certificate in AML, ICA Diploma in AML, and ICA Diploma in Governance, Risk & Compliance. Well-recognized in the UK, EU, and Middle East.

NISM Certifications (India)

Provider: National Institute of Securities Markets (SEBI subsidiary)

Relevant certifications include the NISM Investment Adviser (Series X-A/X-B), Equity Derivatives (Series VIII), and Mutual Fund (Series V-A). While not AML-specific, they demonstrate financial market knowledge valued in Indian compliance roles.

Other Relevant Certifications

CGSS (Certified Global Sanctions Specialist by ACAMS) — for sanctions-focused roles
CFE (Certified Fraud Examiner by ACFE) — for fraud investigation roles
FRM/CFA — for risk management or broader financial analysis roles with compliance elements

Interview Prep

Common fresher interview topics

Based on publicly available interview preparation guides and the author's experience with financial services hiring processes, these are the topics most commonly asked in entry-level AML interviews:

📋 Must-Know Topics

  • What is money laundering? Explain the three stages.
  • What is KYC/CDD? What's the difference between CDD and EDD?
  • What is a PEP? Why do PEPs require enhanced scrutiny?
  • Explain the Three Lines of Defence model.
  • What is a SAR/STR? What is "tipping off"?
  • What is FATF? What are the 40 Recommendations?
  • What is the FATF Grey List / Black List?
  • Name some common money laundering typologies / red flags.
  • What is beneficial ownership? Why does it matter?
  • What is sanctions screening? Name the major sanctions lists.

🎯 Scenario-Based Questions

  • "A customer deposits $9,500 three days in a row. What would you do?"
  • "A PEP wants to open a private banking account. Walk me through the process."
  • "You receive a transaction monitoring alert. Describe your investigation workflow."
  • "A customer's business doesn't match their transaction patterns. What are the red flags?"

💡 Pro Tip Practice answering these questions out loud. Use the quizzes on this site to test yourself. Frame your answers using the "STAR" method (Situation, Task, Action, Result) for behavioural questions.

Career Tips

Resume tips for AML freshers

  • Lead with relevant coursework and certifications: MBA Finance, NISM certifications, CAMS preparation, ICA courses — list them prominently.
  • Highlight simulations and virtual experiences: JPMorgan, Bank of America, Bloomberg market concepts simulations demonstrate practical interest. List them as "Virtual Experience Programs" with specific skills gained.
  • Use compliance keywords: KYC, CDD, EDD, PEP screening, transaction monitoring, SAR/STR, sanctions screening, risk-based approach, FATF, BSA. ATS (Applicant Tracking Systems) screen for these.
  • Quantify where possible: "Analysed 50+ customer profiles during internship" is stronger than "assisted with KYC work."
  • Show continuous learning: Mention this website, relevant books, compliance newsletters you follow, or ACAMS webinars attended.
  • Tailor for each application: Read the job description carefully. Mirror the language and requirements they list.
